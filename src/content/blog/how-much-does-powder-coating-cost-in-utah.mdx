**Short answer:** most powder coating jobs in Utah are priced by the size and
number of parts, the prep required, and the color or finish you choose — which is
why the only accurate number comes from a quick, free quote. Here's how each
factor works so you can budget with confidence.

## What drives the price

- **Size and quantity.** A single set of wheels costs far less than a trailer
  frame or a batch of railings. More surface area means more powder and more oven
  time.
- **Prep work.** Parts that arrive rusty or painted need [sand or shot
  blasting](/sand-shot-blasting/) first. Clean bare metal coats faster and cheaper.
- **Color and finish.** Standard colors are the most economical; specialty
  textures, metallics, candies, and two-tone finishes add cost.
- **Disassembly and masking.** Threads, bearing surfaces, and areas that must stay
  bare take extra time to mask.
- **Turnaround.** Standard scheduling is included; rush jobs may carry a premium.

## Typical examples

While every job is quoted individually, powder coating is usually very
cost-competitive with a quality paint job — and it lasts far longer. A set of
wheels, a UTV cage, patio furniture, or a railing are all common jobs we turn
around quickly.

## Why powder coating is worth it

Powder coating commonly lasts **15–20 years**, resists chips and fading, and needs
almost no maintenance. Spread over its lifespan, it's typically *cheaper* than
repainting — and it looks better the whole time. See the full breakdown on our
[powder coating page](/powder-coating/).
