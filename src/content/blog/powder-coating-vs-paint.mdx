**Short answer:** powder coating lasts longer and holds up better than liquid
paint in almost every outdoor and high-wear situation — which is why we finish our
ironwork with it in-house.

## How they differ

| | Powder coating | Liquid paint |
|---|---|---|
| **Durability** | Hard shell, resists chips & scratches; ~15–20 yrs | Softer film; chips and fades sooner |
| **Finish** | Thick, even, no runs or drips | Can run, sag, or show brush/spray marks |
| **Color & texture** | Hundreds of colors + textures (matte, gloss, hammered) | Wide colors, fewer textures |
| **Eco-friendly** | No solvents/VOCs; overspray is reclaimable | Solvent-based, more VOCs |
| **Corrosion resistance** | Excellent, especially over blasted metal | Good, but more prone to edge failure |

## When paint still makes sense

Paint can be the right call for surfaces that can't go in a curing oven (large
fixed structures, heat-sensitive materials, or wood). For most metal — wheels,
railings, gates, furniture, parts — powder coating wins on longevity and looks.
