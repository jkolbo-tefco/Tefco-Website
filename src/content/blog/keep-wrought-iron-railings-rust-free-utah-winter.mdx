**Short answer:** keep iron railings rust-free by starting with a powder-coated
finish, rinsing off road salt, and touching up nicks before winter. Utah's
freeze-thaw cycles and de-icing salt are hard on bare or painted iron — but easy
to manage.

## Why Utah winters are tough on iron

Snowmelt, road salt, and repeated freezing and thawing work moisture into any chip
or scratch. Once bare steel is exposed, rust spreads under the finish. The fix is
mostly about keeping that protective layer intact.

## Five steps to a rust-free railing

1. **Start with powder coating.** A [powder-coated](/powder-coating/) railing has a
   hard, sealed shell that resists chips far better than paint. This is the single
   biggest factor.
2. **Rinse off salt.** A few times each winter, hose down railings near
   driveways and walkways to remove de-icing salt.
3. **Inspect for chips.** Look for nicks where snow shovels or furniture may have
   scraped the finish. Bare spots are where rust starts.
4. **Touch up early.** Clean and touch up small chips before winter — or bring the
   piece to us for a fresh finish.
5. **Keep it clean.** A wipe-down with mild soap and water (no abrasives) keeps the
   finish sound for decades.

## When to refinish

If you see surface rust spreading or the finish flaking, it's time to refinish.
We can [blast](/sand-shot-blasting/) the railing back to bare metal and powder
coat it like new — often cheaper than replacing it.
